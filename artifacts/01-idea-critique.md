# Devil's Advocate: Idea Review (Round 2)

## Core Understanding (Modified Design)

The experiment proposes to test whether transformer middle layers encode a format-agnostic semantic substrate ("protocol layer") by extracting hidden states from Qwen3.5-27B and Llama-3.1-8B across 200 text stimuli (40 products x 5 registers), now with a **40-class individual product probe as the primary analysis**, **fictional products as a memorization control**, **anisotropy correction (mean centering + whitening) before cosine similarity**, **a full-precision Qwen3.5-27B run on a subset as a quantization control**, **a pre-registered topic-modeling null hypothesis**, and **diversified stimulus generation sources** (Claude + GPT-4 + human-written controls). The three nested hypotheses (H1: phase structure, H2: content dominance, H3: protocol layer advantage) remain, but the evidentiary bar has been raised substantially.

## Issues Resolved by Round-1 Modifications

The modifications address the most critical design flaws from round 1 effectively. The 40-class primary probe eliminates the trivial-separability problem that would have made the original 8-class results uninterpretable. The fictional product condition is a clean memorization control. Anisotropy correction removes the most dangerous measurement artifact. The full-precision Qwen subset run makes quantization effects diagnosable rather than confounded. These are substantive improvements that move the design from "likely to produce uninterpretable positive results" to "capable of producing meaningful evidence."

## Remaining and New Challenged Assumptions

1. **"Five samples per class (one per register) is sufficient for a 40-class linear probe."** This is the new central statistical problem created by the modification. With 200 stimuli divided into 40 classes, you have exactly 5 samples per product. A 40-class logistic regression with 5-fold CV means each fold has 160 training samples and 40 test samples, with 4 training examples and 1 test example per class. This is a few-shot classification regime where probe accuracy will be dominated by variance, not signal. Confidence intervals will be enormous. A probe that achieves 30% accuracy on 40 classes (vs. 2.5% chance) sounds impressive, but with 40 test samples per fold, the standard error of accuracy is roughly sqrt(0.3 * 0.7 / 40) = 7.2 percentage points. Distinguishing "middle layers are better by 5pp" from noise requires far more data per class than this design provides.

2. **"Fictional products are an adequate memorization control."** The control assumes memorization operates at the product-identity level (the model has seen "Crest Pro-Health" before). But memorization could also operate at the category-feature level -- the model has encoded that "sodium fluoride" clusters with toothpaste regardless of brand. A fictional toothbrush with "UV-C sterilization" still uses concepts the model has encountered in oral care contexts. The fictional condition controls for brand memorization but not for conceptual-cluster memorization. To truly isolate protocol-layer processing from memorization, you would need products whose features have no pretraining-data association with their category -- which may be impossible to construct while keeping stimuli naturalistic.

3. **"Diversifying generation sources (Claude + GPT-4 + human) controls for stimulus contamination."** This introduces a new confound: generator identity. If Claude-generated stimuli cluster differently from GPT-4-generated stimuli in hidden-state space, you now have a generator effect entangled with your product/register effects. Unless you ensure every product has stimuli from multiple generators (not just "some products from Claude, some from GPT-4"), and unless you explicitly test for generator effects in the probe analysis, diversification could make things worse rather than better. The question is: what is the experimental design for the multi-source generation? Is it crossed (every product x every register x every generator) or nested?

4. **"Mean centering + whitening is sufficient anisotropy correction."** Per-layer whitening assumes the anisotropic bias is a global, linear distortion of the representation space. If anisotropy varies by token frequency, sequence length, or register type (e.g., patent language occupies a different region of the hypercone than casual language), global whitening may under-correct in some subspaces and over-correct in others. More critically, whitening transforms the representation geometry -- it can amplify noise dimensions that were suppressed in the original space. If a small number of principal components carry most of the variance (common in highly anisotropic representations), whitening will inflate the contribution of low-variance components, potentially introducing noise that was harmless before correction.

5. **"The full-precision subset run adequately controls for quantization effects."** What subset? If you run 50 of the 200 stimuli at full precision, you get partial coverage. The comparison becomes: does the per-layer similarity/probe-accuracy curve have the same *shape* at full precision vs. 4-bit? This is a qualitative visual comparison, not a statistical test. You need to pre-specify what "quantization does not distort the phase structure" means quantitatively. How different can the curves be before you conclude quantization is a problem?

6. **"Register variation within English is sufficient to stress-test format-agnosticism."** This was raised in round 1 but not fully resolved. The modifications address *measurement* confounds (anisotropy, memorization, easy task), but the *conceptual* question remains: English registers share 90%+ of vocabulary, all of grammar, and most syntactic structures. If middle layers strip away the 10% that differs, this is a mild normalization -- not evidence of a deep "protocol layer" that handles cross-lingual or cross-modal transfer. The modifications make the experiment better at what it measures, but the gap between what it measures and what it claims remains.

## Remaining and New Gaps

1. **Statistical power for the 40-class probe is critically undefined.** This is the single most important gap. The round-1 modification correctly elevated 40-class to primary, but the design was built around 200 stimuli -- a number that made sense for 8 classes but is dangerously underpowered for 40. You need either: (a) more stimuli per product (e.g., 10 registers or paraphrases per product, yielding 400 stimuli), (b) a formal power analysis showing 5 samples/class is sufficient given expected effect sizes, or (c) an alternative analysis strategy that does not require per-class sample sizes (e.g., metric learning or representational similarity analysis that operates on pairwise distances rather than classification). Without this, the 40-class probe may produce null results simply due to insufficient power, making H2 and H3 unfalsifiable in practice.

2. **No specification of how fictional products integrate into the analysis.** If you add, say, 20 fictional products (5 per category? spread across categories?) with 5 registers each, that is 100 additional stimuli. Does the 40-class probe become a 60-class probe? Are fictional products analyzed separately as a parallel experiment? What is the pre-registered comparison: "fictional products show the same phase structure as real products" or "fictional products show *any* phase structure"? The design needs a concrete integration plan, not just "a parallel condition."

3. **No pre-registered analysis plan for the multi-source stimulus comparison.** If stimuli come from Claude, GPT-4, and humans, you need to specify: (a) how many stimuli from each source, (b) whether sources are crossed with products/registers or nested, (c) what statistical test determines whether source is a confound, and (d) what happens if source *is* a significant predictor. Without this, the diversification is ad hoc and cannot be interpreted rigorously.

4. **The topic-modeling null hypothesis needs sharper discriminating predictions.** The decision log says "if the 40-class probe shows protocol-layer advantage but the 8-class probe does not (or vice versa), this distinguishes semantic identity from coarse topic clustering." But the more likely outcome is that *both* show some advantage, with the 8-class probe showing a larger advantage (more signal per class). In that case, the topic-modeling null and the protocol-layer hypothesis make the same prediction, and the null is not discriminated. A sharper discriminating criterion would be: "if the protocol-layer advantage for within-category product discrimination (e.g., distinguishing Crest from Colgate from Sensodyne) is as large as the advantage for cross-category discrimination, this favors the protocol-layer hypothesis over topic modeling."

5. **No plan for handling the residual stream vs. component decomposition.** If middle-layer hidden states show the predicted pattern, is it because MLP outputs encode semantic content, attention outputs encode register information, or both contribute? Decomposing hidden states into attention output, MLP output, and residual stream contributions at each layer would add minimal computational cost (the forward hooks can capture these separately) and would substantially enrich the analysis. This was raised in round 1 but not addressed.

6. **CPU offloading for full-precision Qwen3.5-27B is not characterized.** A 27B parameter model at FP16 requires ~54GB of VRAM. With 32GB on the 5090, you need ~22GB offloaded to CPU RAM. CPU offloading dramatically slows inference (10-50x for the offloaded layers) and changes the computational graph for mixed-device execution. Have you verified that HuggingFace's device_map="auto" with output_hidden_states=True correctly returns all hidden states when layers are split across GPU and CPU? Some implementations only return hidden states from device-local layers. This needs a pilot test.

7. **Stimulus length control remains underspecified.** The target is 80-150 tokens, but real register variation produces naturally different lengths. A patent claim for a toothbrush might be 200+ tokens; a tweet is 30. If you enforce 80-150 tokens, patent and social stimuli will be unnaturally truncated or padded, destroying register authenticity. If you allow natural length variation, mean pooling over 30 vs. 200 tokens produces vectors with very different noise profiles (the 30-token mean is much noisier). You need a principled decision here, not a soft target range.

## Remaining and New Risk Scenarios

1. **The 40-class probe produces noisy, uninterpretable results due to insufficient samples per class (HIGH likelihood, HIGH impact).** With 5 samples per class in a 40-class problem, stochastic variation in the train/test split will dominate. You may see the "protocol layer advantage" appear or disappear depending on which sample ends up in the test fold. This would make H2 and H3 practically untestable with the current sample size. This risk is a direct consequence of the (correct) decision to elevate 40-class to primary without increasing the stimulus count.

2. **Generator effects create an uncontrolled confound that mimics or masks the protocol-layer signal (MEDIUM likelihood, HIGH impact).** If Claude-generated patent text and GPT-4-generated patent text for the same product differ systematically in hidden-state space, the generator becomes a nuisance variable that interacts with both product and register. Without a fully crossed design (every product x register from every generator), you cannot estimate or remove this effect. If generator effects are large, they could either inflate the product-clustering signal (if Claude generates all registers for product A and GPT-4 generates all registers for product B) or suppress it (if generators are crossed with registers).

3. **Full-precision Qwen subset run fails or is impractically slow (MEDIUM likelihood, MEDIUM impact).** CPU offloading for 27B at FP16 on 32GB VRAM + 32GB RAM is at the edge of feasibility. If the offloaded run takes 10+ hours for the subset, or if hidden-state extraction fails across the device boundary, you lose the quantization control. Fallback plan: run Qwen at 8-bit quantization as an intermediate control (fits in 32GB VRAM comfortably), which at least narrows the quantization gap.

4. **Within-category product discrimination fails at all layers, but cross-category discrimination succeeds -- and you cannot tell if this falsifies H2/H3 or just shows that within-category products are genuinely similar (MEDIUM likelihood, MEDIUM impact).** If Crest Pro-Health and Colgate Total have near-identical hidden states in middle layers, is this because the protocol layer correctly identifies them as "similar products" (supporting the hypothesis) or because the model cannot distinguish them (failing to support fine-grained semantic encoding)? The 40-class probe conflates discriminability with semantic distance. Products that *should* be similar will look like probe failures.

5. **Whitening destroys the phase structure (LOW likelihood, HIGH impact).** If the anisotropy IS the signal -- if the bell-shaped anisotropy curve in middle layers reflects the compression into a protocol-layer representation -- then correcting for it could flatten the very effect you are trying to measure. You would correctly remove the artifact but incorrectly conclude H1 is falsified. Consider running the analysis both with and without anisotropy correction and reporting both.

## Key Questions for the User

1. **How will you handle the statistical power problem for the 40-class probe?** With 5 samples per class, the probe is in a few-shot regime where variance dominates signal. Have you considered: (a) increasing to 10+ registers/paraphrases per product, (b) using representational similarity analysis (RSA) or centered kernel alignment (CKA) instead of classification accuracy, or (c) bootstrapped confidence intervals on pairwise distances rather than probe accuracy? -- *Priority: HIGH*

2. **What is the concrete experimental design for multi-source stimulus generation?** Is it fully crossed (every product x register x generator), or will different generators handle different products? If not fully crossed, how will you estimate and remove generator effects? -- *Priority: HIGH*

3. **How do fictional products integrate into the quantitative analysis?** How many fictional products, in how many categories, with what probe design? Is the fictional-vs-real comparison a separate analysis or folded into the main 40-class probe? What is the specific pre-registered prediction? -- *Priority: HIGH*

4. **What is the quantitative criterion for "quantization does not distort the phase structure"?** Is it correlation > 0.9 between full-precision and 4-bit per-layer probe curves? Visual similarity? A specific statistical test? Without this, the quantization control is subjective. -- *Priority: HIGH*

5. **Have you piloted the full-precision Qwen3.5-27B run with CPU offloading?** Specifically: does output_hidden_states=True return all layers when the model is split across GPU and CPU? What is the wall-clock time per stimulus? Is this feasible for even a subset? -- *Priority: HIGH*

6. **Will you run the cosine similarity analysis both WITH and WITHOUT anisotropy correction?** If the correction flattens the H1 curve entirely, that is an important result -- but you need the uncorrected baseline to interpret it. -- *Priority: MEDIUM*

7. **How will you handle the semantic similarity confound in the 40-class probe?** Products within a category may be genuinely similar (Crest vs. Colgate). Low within-category discrimination accuracy at middle layers could mean the protocol layer is *correctly* representing them as similar, not that it fails to encode product identity. Will you use a graded similarity measure alongside the classification probe? -- *Priority: MEDIUM*

8. **What is the exact stimulus length strategy?** Fixed length (truncation/padding) sacrifices register authenticity. Variable length creates noise-profile confounds in mean pooling. A principled middle ground (e.g., targeting 100 +/- 20 tokens per stimulus, discarding outliers) needs to be specified. -- *Priority: MEDIUM*

9. **Will you decompose hidden states into attention output, MLP output, and residual stream?** This adds minimal computational cost and could reveal which computation drives the protocol-layer effect (if it exists). Is this in scope? -- *Priority: MEDIUM*

10. **What sharper criterion distinguishes the topic-modeling null from the protocol-layer hypothesis when both 8-class and 40-class probes show advantages?** The current discriminating criterion (one succeeds, the other doesn't) covers an unlikely outcome. What about the more likely case where both show some advantage? -- *Priority: MEDIUM*

## Alternative Perspectives

### Representational Similarity Analysis as Primary Method

Given the severe sample-size constraint for the 40-class probe, consider making **Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA)** the primary analytical method instead of classification probes. RSA operates on pairwise distance matrices rather than per-class accuracy. With 200 stimuli, you have 19,900 pairwise distances -- far more statistical power than 40 classes with 5 samples each. You construct a "model RDM" (representational dissimilarity matrix) reflecting your hypothesis (same product = low distance, same category = medium, different category = high) and correlate it with the observed RDM at each layer. This directly tests whether middle-layer geometry matches the predicted semantic structure without requiring per-class sample sizes. RSA is standard in computational neuroscience for exactly this reason and has been applied to transformer representations (Abnar et al., 2020; Merchant et al., 2020). It would let you keep the 200-stimulus design while gaining the statistical power you lost by moving to 40 classes.

### Graded Similarity Rather Than Hard Classification

The protocol-layer hypothesis predicts a specific *geometry* -- same-product representations should be closest, same-category next, different-category most distant. A classification probe reduces this rich geometric prediction to a single accuracy number. Consider testing the full geometric prediction: at each layer, compute the rank correlation between (a) the predicted similarity structure (same-product > same-category > different-category) and (b) the observed pairwise distances. This tests H1, H2, and H3 simultaneously in a single, more powerful analysis.

## Overall Assessment

The round-1 modifications meaningfully strengthened the design. The fictional product control, anisotropy correction, quantization control, and 40-class primary probe each address genuine weaknesses. The modified design is substantially better than the original.

However, the modifications have introduced a **new critical weakness**: the 40-class probe with 5 samples per class is statistically underpowered. This is now the single biggest risk to the experiment -- bigger than any remaining confound -- because it threatens to make the primary analysis uninterpretable regardless of whether the hypothesis is true. The experiment was designed around N=200 for an 8-class task; the correct fix to "make the task harder" was to increase classes, but the sample size was not increased to match.

The second major remaining issue is **underspecification of the modifications**. The decision log says "add fictional products," "diversify generation sources," "run full-precision subset," but none of these have concrete experimental designs attached. How many fictional products? What is the multi-source generation crossing structure? What subset for full precision? What quantitative criteria for the quantization control? These details matter for whether the modifications actually work or just look good on paper.

**Is this ready for detailed planning?** Almost. The conceptual design is sound. Before moving to planning, you need to resolve two things: (1) the statistical power problem, either by increasing stimulus count or switching to a pairwise distance-based analysis method (RSA/CKA), and (2) concrete specifications for the fictional product condition, multi-source generation design, and quantization control criteria. Once those are pinned down, the design is ready to plan.
